How Escrow Protects Every Trade on Sphere

Trade Sphere Marketplace team · 6 min read

Cross-border trade has always carried a simple problem: a buyer doesn't want to pay before goods arrive, and a seller doesn't want to ship before payment clears. Escrow exists to solve exactly that standoff, and it's the foundation every transaction on Trade Sphere Marketplace is built on.

What actually happens at checkout

When a buyer pays for a listing, funds move into a secure holding state rather than straight into the seller's account. The seller is notified to fulfill the order, and the buyer is notified once it's on the way. Funds are only released to the seller once the buyer confirms the order arrived as described.

If something goes wrong — the item doesn't arrive, or doesn't match the listing — the buyer can open a dispute before funds are released, giving Sphere's support team a chance to step in.

Why this matters more for cross-border trade

Domestic transactions usually have some recourse: a local court, a shared bank regulator, a card network's chargeback process. Cross-border trade between, say, a buyer in Accra and a seller in Nairobi often has none of that in practice. Escrow rebuilds that missing trust layer directly into the transaction.

  • Sellers know a real, committed buyer is on the other end before they ship.
  • Buyers know their money isn't gone the moment they click pay.
  • Disputes have a clear, neutral process instead of an awkward back-and-forth over chat.

What sellers should know

Escrow isn't a delay tactic — most funds release automatically a few days after delivery confirmation if no dispute is raised. Keeping tracking information and delivery photos on hand speeds up any dispute resolution considerably.

Trust isn't a promise on Sphere. It's a mechanism. Escrow is the clearest example of that in action.
