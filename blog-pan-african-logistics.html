Pan-African Logistics, Explained

Trade Sphere Marketplace team · 7 min read

Shipping a product from Lagos to Nairobi involves more moving parts than shipping between two cities in the same country: different customs regimes, currencies, carriers, and paperwork requirements at every border crossed.

The core challenge: fragmentation

Africa doesn't have a single unified logistics network the way, say, the EU's single market does. A seller who wants to reach buyers in multiple countries has traditionally needed separate relationships with separate freight brokers in each corridor — expensive, slow, and hard to track consistently.

What "pan-African logistics" actually means in practice

  • Consolidated freight partners who already operate across multiple regional corridors, instead of one relationship per country.
  • Standardized customs documentation generated once per listing category, rather than re-created per shipment.
  • Unified tracking, so a buyer in one country and a seller in another see the same shipment status instead of relying on WhatsApp updates.

Why this matters for smaller sellers specifically

Large exporters have long had the scale to negotiate their own cross-border freight deals. Independent sellers and small businesses haven't — which has meant continental trade was effectively closed to them unless they partnered with a bigger distributor. Pooled logistics infrastructure changes that math by spreading the same rates and reliability across many smaller shipments.

What to expect as a seller

Delivery estimates will vary more by corridor than by distance — a shipment between two coastal capitals with good port infrastructure can move faster than one to a landlocked interior region, even if it's geographically closer. Building that expectation into your listing's delivery estimate up front avoids most buyer frustration.

Cross-border trade in Africa is still catching up to its own potential. Logistics — quietly, unglamorously — is the piece that makes the rest of it possible.
